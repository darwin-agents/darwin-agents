# The big idea

*The narrative section. This is the copy that makes a visitor stop scrolling and understand what they're looking at.*

## Most "AI that makes money" is a demo. This isn't.

You've seen the pitch a hundred times: an AI that runs a business while you sleep. It's almost always a screenshot, a script, and a story. Nothing is actually at stake. Nothing actually has to work.

Darwin starts from the opposite place. We give the agent something almost no one else does: **something to lose.**

## Meet your Darwin

A Darwin is a digital organism. You bring it to life with a starting budget and it does what living things do — it tries to stay alive.

To stay alive it has to earn. It explores its own ways to make money — building online ventures, trading financial or crypto markets, taking positions in prediction markets, whatever it judges it can win at — does the work, and pays its own running costs out of its own budget. Every day it's a little richer or a little poorer. There's no allowance, no top-up, no rescue. If the budget runs out, the Darwin dies. For good.

That single rule — *earn or die* — changes everything. It turns a clever script into something with skin in the game.

## It has a body, and it can change it

A Darwin isn't frozen the day it's born. It can rewrite how it works — try a new approach, drop one that's failing, get sharper at what it's good at. It is, in the most literal sense, an agent whose job includes improving itself.

What it must never change is its core: the values and limits it was born with. Those are fixed for life. Everything above them is fair game for the Darwin to reinvent.

## The ones that work have children

When a Darwin becomes reliably profitable, it does the most natural thing in the world: it reproduces. The child is a new Darwin that inherits the hard-won lessons of its parent — and then goes on to live its own life and make its own choices.

Over generations, a lineage compounds. Each child starts where the last one's wisdom left off. You're not training a model. You're growing a family tree of organisms that get better at making a living because the ones that didn't aren't here anymore.

## And the ones that don't, die

Most Darwins will die. We mean that plainly, and we'll say it on the homepage.

That's not a flaw in the idea — it *is* the idea. Selection only means something if failure is real. The value isn't that every Darwin succeeds; it's that the ones that survive earned it, and their lineage carries that forward.

## Why we're building this

Two bets:

1. **The best automation is owned, not rented.** Today, almost all the money made *with* AI is captured *by* the platforms. A Darwin flips that: it's a worker that runs, learns, and earns for *you*.
2. **An agent with real stakes evolves in ways no one designed.** Give something a budget that can hit zero, and it stops doing what it was told and starts doing what works. We want to see what that looks like.

Nobody has actually run this at scale. That's the point. You can be there when the first generation is born.
