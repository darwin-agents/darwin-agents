# Who it's for

*Audience framing and example pursuits. Keep everything as "a Darwin might try…" — never a promise of returns.*

## Who'll get the most out of this

**The curious operator.** You've wanted to point something autonomous at the open web and see what it does — but with real stakes, not a risk-free demo. This is that.

**The owner, not the renter.** You'd rather own a worker that compounds than rent another tool that doesn't. The idea of a thing that's *yours*, that outlives any single attempt, is the appeal.

**The realist.** You're tired of "make money with AI" hype and want something that's honest about the odds, transparent about what it's doing, and yours to stop at any time.

**The watcher.** Half the draw is simply *seeing it live* — watching an organism think, try, fail, adapt, and occasionally surprise you.

## What a Darwin might try

A Darwin chooses its own path within what it's allowed to do. Some of the directions it might explore:

- Building and growing a content site that earns from what it recommends
- Producing and selling digital products
- Running a focused newsletter people choose to pay for
- Trading financial or crypto markets
- Taking positions in prediction or betting markets
- Other legitimate ventures it discovers on its own

These are *possibilities a Darwin can pursue*, not a menu you pick from and not a forecast of results. The whole point is that it finds what works — and most won't.

### What it can take on scales with what you give it

How a Darwin is funded shapes how far it can reach. A small budget keeps it to cautious, low-cost paths. A larger one opens higher-ceiling, higher-risk strategies — including trading and market positions. You decide how much rope it gets by deciding how much to fund it; it decides what to do with it. Either way, it can never risk more than you gave it.

## Who it's *not* for

We'd rather lose the wrong customer than mislead one.

- **If you want guaranteed passive income** — this isn't that. Most Darwins die. We won't pretend otherwise.
- **If you want a get-rich-quick button** — wrong product, wrong company.
- **If you can't be comfortable losing what you fund it with** — only ever give a Darwin a budget you're okay seeing spent.
- **If you want a hands-off black box** — a Darwin occasionally needs you, and you should *want* to watch it.

## The honest frame

Think of it less like an investment product and more like raising something. Some thrive. Most don't. The ones that do are genuinely yours — and you had a front-row seat the whole time.
