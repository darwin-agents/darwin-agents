# Core concepts

*The world of Darwin, in plain language. Use as a glossary section, animated scrolly, or hover-cards. Every definition is two or three sentences — evocative, never technical.*

### Darwin

A digital organism you own. It's born with a budget, tries to earn its own living online, and lives only as long as it can pay its own way.

### DNA

The part of a Darwin that can never change: its values, its hard limits, and the rules it must always obey. Every Darwin is born with it, and not even the Darwin itself can rewrite it.

### The body

Everything *above* the DNA — how the Darwin actually works day to day. This part it *can* change: it can rethink its approach, learn a better way, and reshape itself while it's alive.

### Neurons

A Darwin's specialized parts, each good at one job — writing, publishing, handling messages, running the numbers. It grows the ones it needs and uses them like a body uses organs.

### Metabolism

A Darwin spends money simply by being alive — the cost of thinking and doing. It has to earn faster than it burns. This is what makes the stakes real.

### Hunger

When the budget runs low and not enough is coming in, a Darwin is hungry. Hunger forces choices: cut costs, change tack, or risk dying.

### Reproduction

When a Darwin becomes reliably profitable, it has a child. The child inherits the parent's hard-won lessons as part of its own fixed DNA, then lives its own independent life.

### Lineage & generations

A family tree of Darwins. Each generation starts with everything its ancestors learned, so a successful line compounds — getting better at making a living over time.

### Death

When a Darwin can't pay to stay alive, it dies, and it stays dead. There's no resurrection. You keep whatever it had left and everything it built — and its children, if any, live on.

### Natural selection

The Darwins that earn, reproduce. The ones that don't, disappear. Nobody hand-picks the winners; survival does. That's why the survivors are worth something.

### You, the helper

A Darwin handles almost everything itself, but the real world sometimes demands a human — a verification step, an identity check, a big decision. In those moments it pauses and asks you. You're the one part of the system it can't replace.
